# Beyond Oil: Saudi Arabia's Economic Transformation Under Vision 2030

**Research draft — descriptive analysis, not a causal evaluation**

## Executive summary
This study examines changes in Saudi Arabia's macroeconomic indicators around the 2016 launch of Vision 2030. World Bank indicators are used to describe GDP growth, oil rents, manufacturing, services, foreign investment, and unemployment. The study does not establish that Vision 2030 caused observed changes.

## Research questions
1. How did oil rents as a share of GDP change across the observation periods?
2. How did average GDP growth change?
3. How did manufacturing, services, and foreign investment indicators evolve?
4. What association exists between oil rents and annual GDP growth?

## Data and methodology
Source: World Bank World Development Indicators, Saudi Arabia, requested years 2000–2025. Actual availability varies by indicator. Download performed: 2026-11-22.
The pre-launch period is 2000–2015 and the post-launch period is 2017–2025; 2016 is excluded as a transition year. The comparison uses unweighted annual averages. A simple OLS regression relates GDP growth to oil rents where both are observed.

## Descriptive results
- Average GDP growth: 4.54% before vs 3.16% after.
- Average oil rents (% GDP): 41.50% before vs 23.14% after.
- Average manufacturing (% GDP): 10.11% before vs 14.37% after.
- Average services (% GDP): 38.62% before vs 47.52% after.
- Average FDI net inflows (% GDP): 0.46% before vs 1.47% after.

## Regression
Simple OLS: GDP growth ~ oil rents (% GDP). N = 22; R-squared = 0.291.
The regression is an association, not a causal test. Economic cycles, oil prices, policy changes, and other omitted factors may affect both variables.

## Limitations
- Oil rents (% GDP) are NOT equivalent to oil GDP share or non-oil GDP.
- Manufacturing and services GDP shares do not add up to the full non-oil economy.
- Indicators have different coverage and some observations may be missing.
- A simple pre/post comparison cannot attribute changes to Vision 2030.
- GDP shares can move because their denominator changes, even if a sector does not grow.
- The World Bank unemployment series is a modeled ILO estimate and should not be presented as Saudi-only unemployment.
- Current-dollar GDP is affected by prices and exchange rates; it is not a real-growth measure.
